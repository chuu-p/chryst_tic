# Scene Ideas

## Meet Cutes & Plot Points

- **Astryd / Paladin Introduction**: A royal paladin lady teenager that has to do a real mission for the first time to be certified as a paladin (shield and sword tank).
- **Tymo and Berlynde**: Tymo has to do a spy/recon mission, almost dies, and they hire Berlynde as a result.
- **Tymo Weapon Aesthetic**: Uzi mit schalldämpfer "sie ist fast so groß wie ich" (because he is a halfling and sniper).
- **Misc Ref**: Twisted fate.
- **Visual Mood**: [Reference Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nfIVcY-YtIY)
- **Yswyndt Origins - The First Death**: A young Chryst witnesses an elder's death in the village. Instead of fear, he feels curiosity. He touches the departing soul and realizes he can feel its warmth. The elder's family watches in horror and awe as the boy speaks to something they cannot see.
- **Yswyndt Origins - Fyrchtegott's Warning**: Fyrchtegott shows Chryst the frozen remains of a failed experiment—a soul trapped in resin, still conscious after decades. "This is what happens when the mechanism breaks," he says coldly. "Do not mistake ambition for wisdom." This is the first crack in Chryst's certainty.
- **Yswyndt War - The Last Prayer**: Before the battle, Chryst holds a final service in the trenches. Soldiers who have never prayed before kneel in the mud. He feels their Fayth for the first time as a collective force—a warm, desperate current. He realizes this power could do more than heal. It could change the rules of the world.
- **Yswyndt War - Berlynde's Loneliness**: A brief interlude showing Berlynde alone in her command tent, surrounded by maps and reports she doesn't trust her subordinates to deliver accurately. She eats cold rations by herself. The player sees the cost of her isolation—the same isolation that will define Chryst if he follows her path.
- **Bysfrost - The Tavern's Secret**: After the tavern confrontation, Chryst discovers that the [[Bysfrost Tavern]] owner is secretly sheltering Maxt deserters in the cellar. These soldiers refused orders to burn a village and are now hunted by their own side. Chryst must decide whether to protect them (risking Maxt retaliation) or turn them away (betraying his own values).
- **Brsykyr - The Baptism Watch**: After baptizing Astryd, Chryst keeps vigil through the night as she shivers in the aftermath. Tymo—still a prisoner at this point—watches from his cell and quietly asks, "Does it hurt, becoming someone new?" Chryst answers honestly: "Yes. But not becoming someone new hurts more."

## Impossible Choices (Avant-Garde Nordic Noir Cinematic)

### Chapter - Yswyndt Origins

- **Idea 1 (The Living Experiment):** Fyrchtegott brings a dying elderly villager to Chryst—her body is failing, but her soul is strong and willing to participate. He asks Chryst to attempt a soul-freezing on a living person, something far more dangerous than the lamb experiment. If it succeeds, Chryst could potentially save anyone from death before it happens. If it fails, he will have tortured a willing person into a fate worse than death—a frozen, conscious soul trapped in resin forever. The villager consents, but Chryst must decide: push the boundaries of his art on a living subject who trusts him, or wait and accept that death will claim her naturally, knowing the knowledge he gains could save countless others later.
- **Idea 2 (The Stolen Scripture):** Chryst discovers that the [[Holy Language]] text he needs to complete his thesis was taken from the monastery by [[Frydemann]] before he fled. The only copy exists in Frydemann's abandoned cabin deep in the frozen wilderness—a journey that would take Chryst away during the coldest week of winter, when the village needs its priests most. Leave the village vulnerable to a spiritual crisis during the longest night to pursue his life's work, or stay and tend to the dying while accepting that his thesis may be incomplete?

### Chapter - Yswyndt War

- **Idea 1 (The Civilian Crossfire):** A Maxt artillery barrage forces the villagers of a border settlement into the trenches—men, women, and children caught between the two armies. Chryst can channel his Fayth to shield them, but doing so drains the defensive wards on the main wall. Protect the innocents and risk breaching the line that holds all of Yswyndt, or hold the wall and watch families die in the crossfire while knowing each death is a soul he might have saved.
- **Idea 2 (Berlynde's Bargain):** During a lull in the fighting, Berlynde sends an emissary with an offer: surrender [[Carolyn]] as a hostage and the Maxt army will withdraw from Yswyndt's borders for one full year. The ceasefire would save hundreds of lives and give Yswyndt time to rebuild. But it means handing over the woman he loves to the general who will likely execute her. Chryst must choose between the pragmatic survival of his homeland and the one person who makes him feel human.

### Chapter - Bysfrost

- **Idea 1 (The Starving Bandit):** After the raid, Chryst captures a young bandit—barely older than a boy, emaciated, fighting for scraps. Interrogation reveals he was a farmer whose land was confiscated by [[Maxt]] occupiers; his younger siblings will starve without him. [[Astryd]] demands execution under Maxt law as an example to deter future raids. Show mercy, induct him into the Cult, and risk the village seeing the faith as weak and ineffective—or carry out the execution and establish authority while killing someone who is, at heart, a victim of the same oppression Astryd once served.
- **Idea 2 (The Mayor's Price):** The [[Bysfrost Mayor]] offers to convert the entire village to the [[Cult of Fayth]] in exchange for Chryst's help rigging the upcoming village council elections in his favor. The Mayor's rival is a Maxt sympathizer who would report Chryst's illegal activities. Accept the corrupt deal and grow the faith's network exponentially in a single stroke—mirroring the very authoritarianism he opposes—or refuse, grow slowly and organically, and risk exposure while the village continues to suffer under the Mayor's heavy-handed rule.

### Chapter - Brsykyr

- **Idea 1 (The Forced Champion):** During tournament preparation, Chryst learns that [[Fexta]] is being coerced by the [[Brsykyr occupational Mayor]]—his wife and children are held as hostages, and Fexta has been ordered to kill Chryst in the arena. If Chryst wins, Fexta's family dies. If Chryst throws the match, the [[Cult of Fayth]] remains illegal and every believer in Brsykyr becomes a target. The only third option is to somehow expose the Mayor's scheme mid-tournament, risking everything on a gambit that could get them both executed.
- **Idea 2 (The Mayor's Alternative):** The [[Brsykyr occupational Mayor]], impressed and terrified by Chryst's power, offers him a shortcut: recognize the Cult of Fayth immediately in exchange for Chryst publicly denouncing [[Astryd]] as a traitor to [[Maxt]] and allowing her execution to proceed. The Mayor needs a political scapegoat to justify the occupation's failures. Sacrifice the woman who has given up everything for him—her name, her nation, her identity—and secure the faith's foothold in the occupied capital, or refuse and risk everything in the arena, knowing that failure means Astryd dies anyway and the Cult is crushed before it begins.

### Chapter - The Crisis of Faith (Mid-Game Sequence)

- **Idea 1 (The Glowing Blade of the Innocent):** The squad captures a terrified, starving twelve-year-old orphaned thief who stole bread from a church offering. When Chryst interrogates him with drawn steel, his [[Spell - Holy Blade]] shines blindingly bright—the Goddess decrees the child's soul is "stained with profound sin" and demands a holy execution. Chryst faces a chilling turning point: Willingly butcher a mundane, starving child simply because his Goddess commands it (maintaining absolute religious purity), or sheath the blade and show radical mercy, intentionally defying the Goddess and risking a severe penalty to his magical power.
- **Idea 2 (The Sinner's Reprieve):** They corner a brutal, sadistic Maxt torturer responsible for burning incredibly innocent villagers alive. To the squad's utter horror, Chryst's holy blade remains completely dull—the Goddess's alien logic does not recognize the torturer's atrocities as a "sin against Fayth". If Chryst spares him to follow his rigid dogma, the torturer will absolutely escape and massacre a nearby settlement of Chryst's own new devotees. If Chryst executes him out of pure human rage and protection, he commits his first active rebellion against his own religion.

### Chapter - Byzantine (The Capitalist Mecca)

- **Idea 1 (The Government Mandate):** The wealthy, corrupt Byzantine government offers Chryst a seductive proposition: They will legally mandate the Cult of Fayth, aggressively forcing millions of citizens to pray and contribute raw soul-energy to Chryst's network. In exchange, they want exclusive back-door access to weaponize the magic for their trade wars. If Chryst accepts the authoritarian shortcut, Carolyn's revival is essentially guaranteed, but his religion becomes a horrifying tool of state oppression. If he declines, he misses out on a big opportunity to grow his network. Chryst declines this offer (correct choice).
- **Idea 2 (The Apple Vendor's Sermon):** Exhausted and buckling under the weight of his expanding magical network, Chryst confronts the legendary [[Frydemann]] in a luxurious Byzantine cafe. Instead of offering profound magical secrets to ease his burden, Frydemann pours Chryst a glass of wine and points out the window to an old woman cheerfully haggling over apples. "She didn't need a miracle to smile today, kid," Frydemann tells him. "Stop trying to carry the sky." Chryst must choose whether to dismiss Frydemann as a cowardly broken man, or internalize the hardest lesson of all: that letting go of his divine obsession and accepting mundane reality might be the only way to save his own mind. He disagrees with Frydemann and will later have a large and important battle with him, this is the foreshadowing.

### Chapter - Hamburg (The Belly of the Beast)

- **Idea 1 (The Emperor's Ultimatum):** In the heart of the Maxt Empire, Tymo is brought before the imposing Maxt Emperor. The Emperor points to a massive map of the fractured, endlessly warring Nordic states, arguing that his brutal conquest is the only way to forge them into a unified shield against the coming darkness. He offers Tymo the chance to lead this forced, bloodless integration as a vassal. Tymo must reject the seductive logic of empire, arguing that true resilience can only grow organically from the people. If Tymo accepts, his homeland is spared further war but becomes an obedient satellite of Maxt tyranny. If he rejects it, he condemns his people to a brutal guerilla defense, but powerfully preserves their soul and freedom. Chryst has an arc here, too. He got the allowance to spread his religion but now the [[Maxt Empire Imperator]] tells him to let them use his network and [[Cult of Fayth]] magic for their war ambitions. If not, they will exile and kill all the [[Cult of Fayth]] followers in the empire. This has to simmer for a while. Chryst finds a good solution at the end. He teaches the aspiring priests of his [[Cult of Fayth]], that want to fight in the war for Maxt just the healing and blessing spells, but no programming, message passing, energy storing, offensive spells, etc. The Imperator knows chryst is playing him but he allows it since he also thinks this is a good political decision for both of them for the future.

### Chapter - The Zenith (Late Game Climax)

- **Idea 1 (The Sacrificial Summoning):** The Fayth network has reached critical mass, but to physically summon the Goddess and achieve ultimate power, Chryst needs a pure vessel to anchor her. Completing his dark descent into the "Lonely God," he must make his bleakest Nordic Noir choice yet: sacrificing one of his loyal squad mates (like Astryd or Mimi). The scene is utterly devoid of glorious heroics—just a chilling, quiet execution in a dark, ice-covered sanctuary. If he proceeds, the squad mate's soul is annihilated, and replaced by the terrifying, cold presence of the Goddess who now inhabits his friend's body.
